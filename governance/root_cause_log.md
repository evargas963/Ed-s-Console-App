# Root-cause log — every defect gets 5 whys, and finding a cause RESTARTS the count

**Law (operator 2026-07-19):** *"The 5 levels of why is universal, it's with everything we do.
If we get to the 2nd why and we find the defect, then the 5-level why starts again, until we
can verify everything is fixed."*

Surface fixes leave landmines. A cause found at why-2 is not the root — it is a new defect,
and a new defect gets its own five whys. The chain terminates only when a why produces no
new defect **and** the fix is verified.


**Boundary vs `governance/unproven_register.md`.** This file holds DEFECTS IN OUR CODE
("why did this break?"). The register holds CLAIMS ABOUT THE WORLD ("is this true of the
market, the data, or the vendor?"). A defect never belongs in the register and a claim
never belongs here. Enforced by `check_no_governance_duplication`.

## Rules

1. Every defect opens an entry here with a `RC-<n>` id.
2. Ask why. If the answer names a **new defect**, record it, fix it, and **restart at why-1
   for that new defect** in its own entry (linked as a child).
3. An entry may close only when: five consecutive whys produce no new defect, AND the fix
   has same-session verification evidence, AND every child entry is closed.
4. `status` is `OPEN` until then. `tools/check_institutional_correctness.py::check_root_cause_log`
   (ENFORCED) blocks commits on any `OPEN` entry past its `due` date.
5. "I didn't check", "the sign was inverted", "I didn't do that right" are **not** root causes.
   They are symptoms of a missing mechanical check — keep asking why until you reach the
   check that was absent, then add it.
